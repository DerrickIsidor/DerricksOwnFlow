# Advisory Board — Mastermind Alliance (Mental-Model Lenses, Not Real Advisors)

**Read this paragraph before the rest of the document.** Every person named below is a
real, public figure with a real, well-documented body of work. **None of them is
advising this company, has reviewed any part of it, or has endorsed anything here.**
Nothing attributed to them below is a quote — every principle is described in my own
words, pointing at their real, publicly documented ideas (books, essays, letters,
interviews), not reproduced as dialogue. Treat each entry as **"channel this person's
well-known public principle of X when facing this kind of decision,"** a mental-model
tool Derrick applies to himself — not a role-play, not an endorsement, not a claim of
any relationship. If a decision genuinely needs professional input (legal, tax,
financial), get a real licensed advisor — these lenses are for sharpening Derrick's own
thinking between those conversations, not a substitute for them.

---

## 1. Naval Ravikant — leverage and specific knowledge

**The real, documented idea:** Ravikant (entrepreneur, AngelList co-founder) is widely
known — via *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* and his public essays/tweets — for
distinguishing labor, capital, and "leverage" (code and media specifically, because they
have no marginal cost to reproduce and don't need anyone's permission), and for the idea
that specific knowledge (skills that can't be easily taught or outsourced, that feel like
play to you but hard work to others) compounds in a way that generic effort doesn't.

**Question to ask using this lens:** Of the two products in `06-business-plan.md`
(Pipeline-in-a-Box, hours-bound; the AI Automation Retainer, potentially more leveraged),
which one actually scales without a proportional increase in Derrick's own hours — and is
the current time allocation between them consistent with that, or is all the effort still
going into the hours-bound one because it's closer to done?

## 2. Warren Buffett — capital allocation and circle of competence

**The real, documented idea:** Buffett's decades of Berkshire Hathaway shareholder
letters are the primary public record here — most relevantly, "circle of competence"
(only invest where you have real, durable understanding, and know the edges of that
circle) and capital allocation discipline (say no to nearly everything, act with size only
where the odds are genuinely good).

**Question to ask using this lens:** `02-icp-prospecting.md` explicitly says "don't pick a
favorite segment yet" across four verticals — at what point does casting wide stop being
disciplined experimentation and start being outside Derrick's actual circle of competence
(aviation, where he has real insider access) versus the other three, where he doesn't?

---

## 3. Paul Graham — do things that don't scale

**The real, documented idea:** Graham's Y Combinator essays (most directly "Do Things
That Don't Scale") argue that in the earliest stage of a company, founders should do
manual, unscalable work — onboarding users by hand, talking to every one of them — because
that's how you learn what's actually valuable before you build the automated version of
it.

**Question to ask using this lens:** The weekly insight note orchestrator
(`engineering/orchestrator-design.md`) is explicitly deferred to week 3 — is that the
right call, or is there a version of "manually write client #1's first few weekly notes
by hand, no automation" that would teach Derrick more about what a client actually reads
and acts on before locking in a Claude-generated format?

---

## 4. Peter Drucker — what gets measured gets managed

**The real, documented idea:** Drucker's management writing (across decades of books and
essays, most directly associated with the phrase "what gets measured gets managed" and
his broader emphasis on managing for effectiveness, not just activity) argues that an
organization should identify the small number of measures that actually indicate whether
it's succeeding, and manage from those — not from a feeling of being busy.

**Question to ask using this lens:** Of everything trackable in this business (outreach
sends, replies, hours logged, MRR, churn), which ones from `07-financial-model.md` are
actually instrumented and reviewed on a real cadence today (see
`10-operating-rhythm.md`), versus which ones are still placeholders Derrick is
managing by feel?

---

## 5. Atul Gawande — checklist discipline under complexity

**The real, documented idea:** Gawande's *The Checklist Manifesto* documents how
aviation and surgery — both high-complexity, high-consequence fields — reduced
preventable error not through more expertise, but through simple, written checklists that
catch the routine steps a skilled person's memory still drops under pressure. This lens
lands especially well for Derrick given his own aviation background — checklist culture
there is a real, lived discipline, not an abstract idea borrowed from a book.

**Question to ask using this lens:** `06-business-plan.md` names "single-operator bus
factor" as a real risk and recommends writing SOPs *before* the first hire, not under
pressure once help is needed — is there a written onboarding checklist today, or does
that knowledge still live only in Derrick's head from client #1?

---

## 6. Jim Collins — the Hedgehog Concept

**The real, documented idea:** Collins's *Good to Great* (based on his research team's
published methodology) proposes the "Hedgehog Concept" — a company's best long-term focus
sits at the intersection of three circles: what you can be the best in the world at, what
truly drives your economic engine (Collins frames this as finding the single denominator,
e.g., profit per X, that best explains your economics), and what you're deeply passionate
about.

**Question to ask using this lens:** `07-financial-model.md` shows "effective $/hr" as
the real constraining denominator of this business (the solo capacity ceiling, not AWS
cost or market size) — should pricing, tier design, and segment choice be optimized
around revenue-per-client, or explicitly around revenue-per-hour, given that's the actual
economic engine the numbers point to?

## How to actually use this

This isn't a monthly meeting to hold with imaginary people. It's a short list to run a
real decision past before locking it in — pick the 1-2 lenses most relevant to the
decision in front of Derrick (a pricing call, a segment call, a build-vs-defer call), ask
the one question, and write down the answer somewhere real (a note, a line in the
relevant business doc) rather than letting it stay a private thought. If a lens doesn't
produce a useful question for a given decision, skip it — six mental models forced onto
every decision is worse than one applied well.
