# ICP + Prospecting Method, Per Segment

Goal for week 1: don't pick a favorite segment yet — run a light version of this method
against 2-3 segments in parallel and let the first real reply (not just an open) tell
you where the actual pain is. Spend real effort only where you get a response.

General rule across all four: you're looking for businesses **too big for a shoebox of
spreadsheets, too small to have hired an analyst**. That's the whole window. Signals of
"in pain" below matter more than headcount — a 6-person shop that's clearly drowning in
manual reporting is a better prospect than a 20-person shop with a tidy back office.

---

## 1. General aviation ops (FBOs, MRO/maintenance shops, flight schools)

**Why this one's got your edge:** you already have real insider access through your
airline job and network — warm intros beat cold outreach everywhere, but especially here
where the industry is close-knit and skeptical of outsiders.

**Qualifying criteria**
- Single-location or small multi-location operator (not a major chain with its own IT).
- Billing/ops split across fuel, hangar, maintenance, and instruction — usually a sign
  they're stitching together QuickBooks + a scheduling tool + paper or Excel.
- Owner-operator or small ops-manager team, not a corporate back office.
- Pain signal: they mention (in conversation, on their site, or in a post) manually
  reconciling fuel sales, hangar rent, or maintenance invoices at month-end.
- Budget signal: they're running billable services (fuel, maintenance, instruction
  hours) — real cash flow exists even if it's thin margin.

**How to find them this week**
- **Your own network first** — literally list every airline colleague, FBO contact, or
  aviation acquaintance you have and DM them directly. This segment is where a warm
  intro is worth 10 cold ones.
- **AOPA Airports Directory** (aopa.org/travel/flight-tools/airports-directory) — public
  directory covering 6,400+ FBOs and airport businesses across the US; searchable by
  airport/region. Use it to build a target list by state or metro.
- **AOPA Flight School Finder** (aopa.org/training-and-safety/learn-to-fly/flight-schools)
  — searchable by state/zip/radius; good for building a flight-school-specific list.
- **NATA — National Air Transportation Association** (nata.aero) — represents ~3,700
  member companies across FBOs, ground support, charter, and maintenance. Member
  directory is member-gated, but their site and committee pages are useful for finding
  company names and for framing your pitch in language this industry already uses.
- **ARSA — Aeronautical Repair Station Association** (arsa.org/membership/members-directory)
  — trade association for Part 145 repair stations (MRO), 400+ member companies, public
  directory. Good, verifiable list of maintenance shops to work through directly.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator filters**: Industry = "Airlines/Aviation"; Company
  headcount = 1-50; Title = Owner, General Manager, Director of Maintenance, Chief
  Flight Instructor, FBO Manager; Geography = your region first (in-person credibility
  helps here).

## 2. Auto repair / vehicle service shops

**Why this one's easy to scale:** textbook recession-resilient, and findable
everywhere — every town has a dozen.

**Qualifying criteria**
- Independent shop or small local chain (2-5 locations), not a franchise with corporate
  reporting already built in.
- Pain signal: runs on a shop-management tool (Tekmetric, Shopmonkey, Mitchell 1, etc.)
  plus a separate accounting tool, with no one connecting the two — owner is eyeballing
  two screens instead of one dashboard.
- Budget signal: multiple bays/technicians, steady walk-in or fleet-account volume —
  cash-heavy business by nature.

**How to find them this week**
- **ASA — Automotive Service Association** (asashop.org, member directory at
  members.asashop.org/find-a-shop) — searchable "find a shop" directory of member
  independent repair shops by location. Real, verifiable, and a good source of names +
  contact starting points.
- **Local/regional**: state-level automotive trade associations (most states have one —
  search "[state] automotive service association") and local Chamber of Commerce
  directories, which usually list auto repair members by name with a website/phone.
- **Google Maps + "auto repair near me" for your metro** — sounds unsophisticated, but
  for a local service business this is genuinely how you build week-1 volume: note
  shops with 4+ bays, active Google reviews (signals real transaction volume), and a
  dated or absent website (signals a business that hasn't modernized its back office).
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator filters**: Industry = "Automotive"; Company headcount =
  5-50; Title = Owner, Shop Manager, Service Manager; Geography = your region.

## 3. Home health / elder care agencies

**Why this one's got real pain, higher trust bar:** scheduling and billing complexity
is genuinely brutal in this industry (caregiver visits, Medicaid/private-pay billing,
compliance documentation), but you'll need a warmer approach — healthcare-adjacent
buyers are more cautious about handing data to an outsider.

**Qualifying criteria**
- Non-medical home care or small licensed home health agency (not a large multi-state
  operator with an existing BI team).
- Pain signal: caregiver scheduling in one tool, billing in another (or in Excel),
  owner/admin manually building reports for referral sources or family updates.
- Budget signal: managing a roster of caregivers/clients (even 15-30 active clients is
  real recurring revenue in this industry) — this is a service business with steady
  billing cycles, which is what recession-resilience is built on here.

**How to find them this week**
- **NAHC state association locator** (statelocator.nahc.org) — the National Association
  for Home Care & Hospice runs a directory of state-level home care/hospice
  associations (e.g., Georgia Association for Home Health Agencies, Texas Association
  for Home Care & Hospice). Go to your state's association page — most list member
  agencies, which gives you a real, verifiable local list plus a "member of [state
  association]" credibility hook for your outreach.
- **NAHC national member database** — nahc.org maintains a searchable database of
  30,000+ member home care/hospice agencies; useful once you've picked a region to
  focus on.
- **State licensing boards** — most states publish a public list of licensed home care
  agencies (search "[state] home care agency license lookup"); this is the most
  complete and verifiable list available, since licensing is mandatory.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator filters**: Industry = "Hospital & Health Care" or
  "Individual & Family Services"; Company headcount = 2-50; Title = Owner,
  Administrator, Office Manager, Director of Care.
- **Approach note**: lead with the trust-building version of outreach (template C/D) —
  this segment responds better to "here's exactly what I'd need access to and what I
  wouldn't touch" than a punchy cold pitch.

## 4. Accounting / tax prep firms

**Why this one's fast to pitch, more competitive:** they sell reporting for a living,
so "connect your data and show it in one place" needs zero explanation — but they also
get pitched analytics tools constantly, so differentiation matters (lead with "built
for your clients," not "built for you," if you want a referral-partner angle instead of
a direct-client angle).

**Qualifying criteria**
- Solo practitioner or small firm (2-10 people), not a regional/national firm with its
  own BI stack.
- Pain signal: firm talks about "helping clients see their numbers" but is still doing
  it manually in Excel or exported PDFs from their accounting software.
- Budget signal: has a book of small-business clients already paying for
  bookkeeping/tax — meaning there's a second angle here beyond selling to the firm
  itself: partnering with them to offer *their clients* a dashboard (referral/white-
  label angle, worth floating once you're talking to one).

**How to find them this week**
- **NSA — National Society of Accountants** (nsacct.org, "Find a Pro" directory at
  connect.nsacct.org/nsacctwww/findapro) — represents 30,000+ independent
  practitioners, mostly sole proprietors and small firms; the "Find a Pro" tool is a
  real, public, searchable directory by location.
- **State CPA societies** — every state has one (search "[state] society of CPAs"),
  most publish member directories; good for a regional list.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator filters**: Industry = "Accounting"; Company headcount =
  1-20; Title = Owner, Managing Partner, Principal; Geography = your region.
- **Approach note**: since this segment already understands reporting, you can be more
  direct/technical in outreach than with home health — but expect a more crowded inbox,
  so a warm or locally-specific angle (same metro, mutual connection) matters more here
  than volume.

## Plus: personal/professional network (run this in parallel to all four, starting today)

This is genuinely your highest-probability channel for client #1 — warm, fast, and free.
- List every airline colleague you know who runs or is involved in a side business
  (rental property, a spouse's shop, a side hustle) — this is often the fastest yes.
- Post the build-in-public LinkedIn announcement (deliverable #4) to your existing
  network — DJ audience and wheelie audience included. You don't need them to buy; you
  need them to know someone who might, and to comment/share so the algorithm shows it
  to more people.
- DM anyone in your existing network who fits any of the four ICPs above, even loosely
  — warm beats cold every time this early.

## Week-1 execution plan (concrete, not aspirational)

1. Day 1-2: build a list of 15-20 real prospects total across 2-3 segments (aim for at
   least 5 from your own network) using the sources above.
2. Day 2: post the LinkedIn announcement.
3. Day 3-5: send outreach (templates in `03-outreach-templates.md`) — warm contacts
   first, cold DMs second, batch of 4-5 per day rather than one big blast.
4. Track replies, not sends — the segment that gets you a real conversation is the one
   worth doubling down on for week 2.
