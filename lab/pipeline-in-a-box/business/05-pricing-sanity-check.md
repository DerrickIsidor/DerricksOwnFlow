## Reading on Derrick's pricing

**Setup fees are reasonable, arguably well-calibrated.**
- Starter's $750 sits below the $1,000–2,000 "simple dashboard" comp, which makes sense
  once he has a reusable pipeline template — the market comps above assume building
  from scratch each time, not a productized/templated build.
- Growth's $2,000 sits at the low end of the "complex build" range ($3,000–10,000),
  again reasonable given templating reduces his actual hours well below the ~100-hour
  assumption behind that market number.
- **Verdict: setup fees don't need to change.** They're priced for a productized,
  repeatable build — not a bespoke one — and that's the right comparison.

**Monthly retainers are underpriced relative to the market, on purpose or not.**
- $300/mo (Starter) and $750/mo (Growth) both sit *below* the $1,000–3,000/mo floor for
  even a boutique solo consulting retainer — despite the fact that ongoing pipeline
  monitoring + a weekly written insight note is real recurring labor, not a
  "set it and forget it" subscription.
- They do sit comfortably *above* pure software subscription pricing ($19–50/mo) — which
  matters, because it signals to the client this is a service with a person behind it,
  not a SaaS tool they could cancel on a whim. That distinction is worth protecting.

## Recommendation (rough, not a hard rule)

**Keep pricing as-is for clients #1–2, explicitly framed as "founding client" pricing.**
For week 1 of a cold-start challenge, being priced to get to *yes* fast is the right
call — the case study and testimonial from client #1 are worth more right now than the
extra $150–450/month. This is the cheapest-version-that-tests-the-hypothesis instinct
applied to pricing: don't optimize monthly margin before you've confirmed anyone will
say yes at all.

**Plan to raise monthly retainers ~20–30% starting with client #3, once there's a case
study to point to** — e.g., Starter to ~$375–400/mo, Growth to ~$900–1,000/mo. That
would land both packages inside the real boutique-consultant retainer band instead of
below it, without requiring a business-model rethink. Leave setup fees where they are;
they're already sized correctly for a templated build.

**Say the word "founding client" or "early pricing" out loud to client #1–2**, in
writing if possible (even just a line in the proposal/invoice). It sets the expectation
that price moves for the next cohort, so nobody feels bait-and-switched later, and it
gives Derrick a real, honest reason to charge more once he has proof.

**One thing to watch, not fix yet:** the weekly insight note is the piece most likely to
be underpriced specifically — it's ongoing analyst-style labor (reading the numbers,
writing something a human will actually read), not infrastructure that runs itself once
built. If it ends up taking more than ~1 hour/week per client once he's actually doing
it, that's the first signal the monthly price needs to move sooner than client #3.
